Rules Jordan gave explicitly on Jul 21, 2026 after reviewing 10 intro drafts:

**No specific technologies in hooks.** Never name React, C#, Angular, TypeScript, GitHub Copilot, etc. in the intro paragraph. General "AI-assisted development tooling" is fine; named tools are not.

**Why:** He said "We don't need to mention specific languages. You're getting too specific in these intros." Applies globally, not per-role.

**How to apply:** If a JD names a specific tool that's also in his stack, the hook can reference the *practice* ("AI-assisted development tooling") but not the tool name.

**Confirmed and widened, Sep 2 2026.** This applies to AI coding tools too, not just
languages and frameworks. The Intro Playbook previously said naming a tool the posting
mentions beats the generic AI hook; that line has been corrected. Jordan on a hook
produced under the old reading: *"your values of using claude ai is exactly where I
excel"* — "it was pretty bad." Two failures in one sentence: it names the tool, and
"exactly where I excel" is the empty phrasing the playbook already rejects.

---

**No "0 to 1 products" language.** He doesn't know what this means; it's PM jargon, not his vocabulary.

**Why:** He flagged it on Owner.com (#1) and Vanta (#2). "I don't know what 0 to 1 products mean, change that."

**How to apply:** Replace with plain language: "spinning up new product surfaces," "building something new," "taking a product from first idea through launch."

---

**No weirdly specific domain claims he can't back.** Don't say "interfaces for diabetes care is exactly where I excel" or "making complex healthcare simple." He hasn't built in those domains. The correct register is the general principle: "I've taken complex systems and created simple and intuitive interfaces."

**Why:** "That's weirdly specific, I've never done an interface for diabetes care." And on Included Health: "Again weirdly specific."

**How to apply:** Keep hooks to capabilities he's demonstrated (complex→simple, end-to-end ownership, AI tooling, scalable architecture) without anchoring them to the company's specific product domain.

---

**No ownership-redundant language in the hook.** Paragraph 2 of the letter already says "I've owned the entire development cycle." Don't echo it in the hook ("owning features end to end from first idea through what customers use").

**Why:** "We talk about ownership too much in it, it sounds redundant." — Owner.com feedback.

**How to apply:** Hook should add something Para 2 doesn't already say (company-specific angle, the "complex→simple" principle, AI tooling practice).

---

**Max 1 "and" per sentence. Keep commas clean.**

**Why:** "Too many 'and' and ','. That's a very long sentence. It should be 2. In the future avoid using more than 1 and in a row. It's not proper grammar to say something and something and something."

**How to apply:** If the hook sentence has "and X and Y and Z," break it into 2 sentences. Use "while" as a connector instead of a 3rd "and" (he suggested "while using AI agent" as the right move for Customer.io).

---

**#3 ClickUp was the model to follow.** He called it "a better example of what to do" —
a general capability claim, no specific tech, no domain-specific boast.

The hook as sent broke two rules that were written after it, so it has been corrected
here rather than left as an example that teaches the mistake. As sent it read "...is
exactly where I excel, and building with AI tooling..." — the empty phrasing the Intro
Playbook rejects, plus a second "and" in the same sentence. Corrected:

> ClickUp's focus on architecting reusable frontend patterns that raise product quality
> and developer velocity is the work I know best. Building with AI tooling every day is
> already how I work.

Same angle, split into two sentences, empty phrase removed.

---

**Zillow is NOT a financial services company.** Don't say "I work in financial services now" as a domain credential for Zillow. They're real estate. ZHL is a loan product inside a real estate platform.
